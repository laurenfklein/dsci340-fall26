# Text as Data
**Emory University / DSCI 340 / Fall 2026**

What does it mean to turn text into data? What are the data-scientific techniques that are commonly employed in order to analyze text? How are they applied in the humanities and social sciences? How are they applied in the world? This course will explore these questions through a sustained semester-long project: training a large language model from scratch. Along the way, we will cover additional methods of text analysis including word and phrase similarity, text classification, and text clustering, as well as methods for creating, cleaning, and processing textual datasets. We will also discuss the economic and environmental issues, as well as the human costs, involved in our increasing reliance on large language models—including but not limited to the model we will create for this class.

Introductory courses in computer science and statistics are recommended (but not required) as prerequisites for this course. You will complete all programming exercises in Python, and all in-class assessments—including the final exam—in writing, by hand. As part of your contribution to the class project, you will be expected to submit documentation in Markdown format, and create and give in-class presentations using slide software of your choosing. You will also be expected to take in-class notes in a notebook that I will provide.
